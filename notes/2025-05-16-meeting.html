    Meeting Notes
    Zack, Julie and Isak
    Friday May 16, 15:15 CEST / 9:15 EST

    1 Discussion

    1.1 Developing a controlled vocabulary

    • Three axes:
      • Topics: What is the paper about? (i.e. diaspora, mobility, gender, ritual, etc)
      • Methods: What methods were applied in the paper? (i.e. spatial analysis, genetics, stable isotope analysis, finds analysis)
      • Kind of contribution (i.e. field report, “standard” scientific paper, review of prior work, opinion)
    • The medium (i.e. journal article, book, chapter in an edited volume, etc) will be identified by CrossRef and does not need to be annotated.
    • It’s ok to assign multiple terms to each record.

    1.2 Coding process

    • A research assistant will be able to start work in September, and will be able to work for four weeks.
    • Limited to review of titles and abstracts, or if there is no abstract, the introductions and conclusions.
    • Since this does not involve comprehensive or open coding, we can just do this in a spreadsheet rather than some specialized QDA software.
    • The research assistant will ideally be a more experienced student in the field of Viking Age archaeology.
    • Zack will schedule weekly check-ins with the research assistant and make himself available to address any methodological questions or concerns.

    1.3 Analysis of references

    • We won’t be able to qualitatively annotate references due to lack of time relative to the corpus’ volume.
    • NLP using scite is not as useful as initially thought.
      • tends to “play it safe” by coding references as neutral mentions when human coders identify a more robust stance (see Bakker et al. 2023).
      • Its corpus is limited to works put out by partnered publishers.
    • Potential alternative to scite, but requires greater knowledge of NLP to implement:
      • https://github.com/davidjurgens/citation-function
      • https://jurgens.people.si.umich.edu/citation-function
    • We may be able to derive a sample based on network statistics, i.e. the most or least central references.

    1.4 Analysis of gender

    • Our intent is to identify citation biases against women.
    • The goal is not to identify gender diversity, which is not supported by our method.

    1.5 Predictions and expectations

    • We’re gonna find sub-clusters within clusters, and bridging clusters.
    • Some sub-clusters will be tight-knit and closed, others very broad and open.
    • Some will be very focused on specific topics.
    • There will be many links across institutions, they are not really that fixed.
    • Some citation chains will emerge that reflect casual observations (just a few examples):
      • Lots of cross-references within the Mälaren valley community.
      • Tight-knit cluster among papers that are concerned with ritual.
      • Major computational cluster at Aarhus.
    • If had a broader time horizon, we might be able to trace “genealogies of affiliation” by tracing how identities change based on the evolution of their collaborative networks.
    • Clustering in methods by “academic upbringing”, which has a localized character
    • We may be able to find associations based on age, but mainly by doing a rough assessment of the n most and least central nodes, rather than keying age into the data.

    1.6 Additional ideas and insights

    • Keeping track of topics independently from citational links enables us to validate whether topics are actually clustered around certain individuals or papers, or whether there are diverse centres.
    • We’ll be able to do some non-network descriptive statistics:
      • Distribution of topics by affiliations of authors.
      • Distribution of topics by number of co-authors.
      • Temporal distributions of genders of lead authors.
      • Gender of lead author by size and genders of their co-authors.

    1.7 Logistics

    • Keep things on GitHub for now.
    • Keep our eyes open for alternative collaborative writing platforms that may emerge over the next few months (including via the archaeo-social collective).
